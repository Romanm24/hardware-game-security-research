# Ethics and Safety Statement

## Research Ethics Framework

**Author:** Roman Mares — SOC Analyst in Training | U.S. Army Veteran  
**Project:** Hardware-Based Game Security Research  
**Date:** 2025

## Core Statement

This research project is conducted exclusively for **educational and defensive cybersecurity purposes**. Every decision made in the design, execution, and documentation of this research was guided by a commitment to ethical practice, responsible disclosure principles, and the foundational cybersecurity ethic of understanding threats in order to defend against them.

This repository does not contain, and will never contain, any of the following:

- Instructions for cheating in any game or online platform
- Guidance for bypassing, disabling, or evading anti-cheat systems
- Step-by-step operational instructions for deploying hardware attack tools
- Techniques for gaining unauthorized access to any system, account, or network
- Content intended to facilitate fraud, deception, or unfair advantage
- Any material that could be directly used to harm other players, platform operators, or system owners

If any content in this repository could be interpreted as providing such guidance, that interpretation is outside the scope and intent of this research, and the author welcomes feedback to correct or clarify the relevant content.

## Research Principles

### 1. Isolation First

All hands-on research was conducted in a fully isolated lab environment. No test activity involved:

- Live game servers or production gaming platforms
- Third-party accounts or systems belonging to other individuals
- Any network with connectivity to external services or live infrastructure
- Hardware introduced to systems owned by others without authorization

The test environment was purpose-built for this research and operated independently of any system or service that could be affected by the research activity.

### 2. Understanding, Not Exploitation

The research questions guiding this project were framed entirely around defense:

- *What does this hardware threat look like from a defender's perspective?*
- *What signals, if any, does this threat produce in system telemetry?*
- *What detection strategies and security controls are relevant?*
- *What are the structural limitations of existing defensive approaches?*

At no point was the research guided by questions of how to successfully execute an attack, evade detection, or defeat a specific security product.

### 3. No Reproduction of Attack Techniques

This project documents the *existence* and *conceptual operation* of hardware-based attack surfaces. It does not reproduce, recreate, or document specific implementation details that would serve as operational guidance for offensive use.

Understanding that DMA hardware can access system memory is foundational security knowledge. The specific technical steps by which a particular device is configured for a particular attack are not documented here, and intentionally so.

### 4. Responsible Communication

Security research carries a communication responsibility. This project aims to:

- Present findings at a level of detail that is useful to defenders without being a practical guide for attackers
- Accurately represent the limitations of defensive controls without exaggerating or sensationalizing risks
- Clearly attribute concepts to established security research where applicable
- Avoid framing that glorifies, normalizes, or encourages offensive hardware use

### 5. No Support for Cheating or Platform Abuse

To be unambiguous on a point of particular importance in this research context:

**This project does not support, encourage, or facilitate cheating in online games or any other platform.** Cheating undermines the integrity of gaming communities, harms other players, violates platform terms of service, and in some jurisdictions may have legal implications. The hardware technologies examined in this research can be and are misused for cheating. That misuse is not the subject of this research — the security implications of these hardware categories are.

The author's interest in this threat landscape is professional: understanding how hardware-level threats challenge endpoint security models is directly relevant to a career in cybersecurity. That professional interest exists entirely separately from any endorsement of how these tools are misused.

## Ethical Framework Reference

This research was conducted with reference to the following ethical frameworks and guidelines:

- **EC-Council Code of Ethics** — commitment to ethical security practice and not facilitating harm
- **ISC² Code of Ethics** — acting honorably, honestly, and responsibly in the security profession
- **Responsible Disclosure Principles** — documenting security issues at a level that informs defenders without enabling immediate offensive application
- **The Menlo Report (2012)** — ethical principles for information and communication technology research, including the principle of minimizing harm and balancing benefits against risks
